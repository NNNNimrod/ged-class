The big idea for today: probability is a ratio, the part you want compared with the total. \[\text{probability}=\frac{\text{what you want}}{\text{all the chances}}\] It is always a number from 0 (it can't happen) to 1 (it will surely happen).

The same 7 moves for every problem

  1. Read it all once
  2. What is it asking?
  3. Givens and keywords
  4. Plan
  5. Pick the operations
  6. Compute
  7. Check the answer form

Problem 1: the vowel tiles

In a word game there are 50 wooden letter tiles in all. 20 of them have a vowel on them. You pull out one tile without looking. What's the chance it shows a vowel?

1Read it. What is it asking?

It wants the probability of getting a vowel tile, written as a fraction, a decimal and a percent.

Why it works

Saying the goal out loud before touching numbers keeps you from solving the wrong question. Here the goal is "chance of a vowel", not "how many vowels".

2Givens and keywords

want: 20 vowel tilestotal: 50 tiles

Keyword: probability. That tells you to build a fraction of favorable over possible.

Why it works

A favorable outcome is the result you're hoping for (a vowel). The possible outcomes are every tile you could pull (all 50). Probability isn't on the formula sheet, so you need to remember this one.

3Plan and set up the fraction

\[\frac{\text{favorable}}{\text{possible}}=\frac{20}{50}\]

Plan: write the fraction → simplify it → turn it into a decimal → turn it into a percent.

Why it works

Every tile has the same chance of being picked, so the chance of a vowel is just "how many vowels" out of "how many tiles". The part you want goes on top and the total goes on the bottom.

4Simplify the fraction

Both 20 and 50 end in 0, so both can be divided by 10:

\[\frac{20}{50}=\frac{20\div 10}{50\div 10}=\frac{2}{5}\]

Can 2 and 5 share any other factor? No, so \(\frac{2}{5}\) is in lowest terms.

Why it works

Dividing the top and bottom by the same number is like cutting a pizza into bigger slices: you have fewer slices, but the same amount of pizza. The value doesn't change.

5Fraction → decimal

Way A, make the bottom 10: 5 × 2 = 10, so multiply the top and bottom by 2.

\[\frac{2}{5}=\frac{2\times 2}{5\times 2}=\frac{4}{10}=0.4\]

Way B, long division: \(2\div 5\). 5 doesn't go into 2, so write "0." and bring down a zero: 5 goes into 20 four times. Answer: 0.4.

Why it works

A fraction bar means "divide". Tenths are the first place after the decimal point, so 4 tenths is written 0.4. Making the bottom 10 or 100 is the fastest hand trick whenever it works.

6Decimal → percent

\[0.4=\frac{4}{10}=\frac{40}{100}=40\%\]

Shortcut: move the decimal point 2 places to the right. 0.4 → 0.40 → 40%.

Why it works

Percent means "per hundred": 40% is 40 out of every 100. So once the bottom is 100, the top number is the percent.

7Check: does it make sense? Is it in all three forms?

Fewer than half of the tiles are vowels (20 is less than 25), so the answer should be a little under 50%. 40% fits.

Problem 1 answer:
\(\frac{2}{5}\)0.440%

Why it works

A quick "is it about right?" check catches upside-down fractions. If you'd gotten \(\frac{50}{20}\), that's more than 1, and a probability can never be more than 1.

Problem 2: the color spinner

A spinner is split into 5 equal parts, and each part is red, white or blue. Someone spins it 40 times and keeps track with tally marks. What is the experimental probability that a spin does NOT land on blue?

1Read it. What is it asking?

The chance of not blue, meaning red or white, based on what really happened in the 40 spins. Give it as a fraction, decimal and percent.

Why it works

Little words like NOT flip the whole question. Circle them when you read. Lots of people find the chance of blue and stop.

2Givens: count the tally marks

Each bundle (4 lines with a slash across) is 5. Count by fives, then add the loose lines.

  • red: 1 bundle = 5
  • white: 1 bundle + 2 = 5 + 2 = 7
  • blue: 5 bundles + 3 = 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 … + 3 = 28

Check the total: 5 + 7 + 28 = 40 ✔ (it matches the 40 spins).

Why it works

Tally marks are grouped in fives so you can skip-count instead of counting every line. Checking that the counts add up to the number of spins proves you read the table right.

3Keyword: experimental, and spot the distractor

Experimental probability uses what actually happened:

\[\frac{\text{times it happened}}{\text{number of tries (trials)}}\]

The "5 equal parts" is a distractor. We don't use it at all, because we aren't predicting. We're using the real results.

Why it works

There are two kinds of probability. Theoretical means what should happen, worked out from the spinner itself. Experimental means what did happen, worked out from the record. The question says "experimental", so the 40 spins are the bottom of the fraction. (We also aren't told how many parts are blue, so the 5 parts couldn't help anyway.)

4Plan: how many spins were NOT blue?

Way A, take blue away: \(40-28=12\)

Way B, add the others: red + white = \(5+7=12\)

Both ways give 12 spins that were not blue. So:

\[\frac{\text{not blue}}{\text{all spins}}=\frac{12}{40}\]

Why it works

Every spin is either blue or not blue. There's no third choice. So the non-blue spins are just everything left over after you take away the blue ones. Getting the same number two different ways is a built-in check.

5Simplify the fraction

12 and 40 can both be divided by 4:

\[\frac{12}{40}=\frac{12\div 4}{40\div 4}=\frac{3}{10}\]

3 and 10 share no other factor, so \(\frac{3}{10}\) is in lowest terms.

Why it works

Choose the biggest number that goes into both, and you're done in one step. If you divide by 2 first you get \(\frac{6}{20}\). That's fine, just divide by 2 again to get \(\frac{3}{10}\).

6Decimal and percent

The bottom is already 10, so you can read the decimal right off: 3 tenths.

\[\frac{3}{10}=0.3\qquad 0.3=\frac{30}{100}=30\%\]

(Long-division check: \(3\div 10\). 10 goes into 30 three times, so the answer is 0.3.)

Why it works

Tenths are the first place after the decimal point. Then multiply the top and bottom by 10 to get per hundred, which is exactly what percent means.

7Check with the "blue" side

Chance of blue: \(\frac{28}{40}=\frac{7}{10}=70\%\). Blue and not-blue together cover every spin, so they must add up to 100%:

\[100\%-70\%=30\%\qquad\text{or}\qquad 1-\frac{7}{10}=\frac{3}{10}\]

Both match our answer ✔

Problem 2 answer:
\(\frac{3}{10}\)0.330%

Why it works

"Blue" and "not blue" split all 40 spins into two groups with nothing missing and nothing counted twice. So their chances add up to the whole: 1, which is 100%. Blue came up a lot, so a small "not blue" answer makes sense.

⚠ Common mistakes

  • Upside-down fraction. The total goes on the bottom: \(\frac{20}{50}\), not \(\frac{50}{20}\).
  • Missing the word NOT. \(\frac{28}{40}=70\%\) is the chance of blue, not the answer.
  • Using the 5 spinner parts. "Experimental" means you use the 40 real spins.
  • Miscounting tallies. Each crossed bundle is 5, not 4. Check that the counts add up to 40.
  • Only one form. These questions want all three: fraction, decimal and percent.
